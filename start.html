How to enter the library

Five books, four hundred essays' worth of argument, a music catalog, a handful of rooms. You do not need all of it. Pick a door.

JH
Justin Helmer
Guide · updated 6 November 2026 · about a seven-minute read

Most people arrive here from a search for a book title, a song, or the word LYGO, and then find a house with more rooms than they planned to visit. That is my fault as much as yours. This page is the doorman: it asks what you came for and points at one door instead of nine.

If you came for the books

Start at The Moonlit Slumber. It is Book I, and the saga is built so that the later volumes only cost what the first one agreed to. Read it slowly; the opening movement is a place rather than a plot, and readers who race it tend to bounce off. When you finish, go to The Shattered Accord and keep going in order.

If you want a shorter commitment first, the essay Reading Eternal Haven in order explains why the order matters and what each volume costs you. If you want the cast without the plot, the cast page is deliberately spoiler-free.

If you came for the essays

The essays are the argument side of the house: craft, music, small systems, and what it means to publish in public. Three good entries depending on your mood:

  • The lantern is still on — why a personal site is a house and not weather. Start here if you are tired of feeds.
  • Empty beats fake — the house rule about naming a miss instead of painting a hit. Start here if you work in software or media.
  • Tap tempo and the stubborn body — why a machine that hears tempo still cannot hear a room. Start here if you make music.

If you came for the music

The catalog is free to hear and lives on a different domain because audio files are heavy: asiancoastline.com/listen.html. The page on this site that explains how a song actually arrives — encode, hash, publish, play — is Music in this house. If you want the license in plain language rather than lawyer language, read the music license.

If you came for the rooms

Some people arrive looking for the interactive work, not the fiction. The honest map is the tools room and the dense one is the systems hub. A short version: the ASCII art studio turns a photo into letters in your browser, the Star Chart is a live public ledger of seals, the witness watches public feeds, and the games are small browser things with local scorecards and no login.

If you came for the author

About is the long version. The short version: I am Justin Helmer. I write the Eternal Haven saga, publish music as Excavationpro, and build small verified systems under the name LYGO. I do not run a newsletter that begs. If you want to know when something ships, the essay index is the changelog, and the contact page reaches me directly.

What this house will not do

It will not autoplay audio at you. It will not put a modal over the first paragraph. It will not ask you to make an account to read a chapter. If an advertisement appears, it appears after you have already been given something to read, never above the title. The reasoning is in Ads and a house that still opens, and the private-data detail is in privacy.

How the site is maintained

Pages carry dates because pages change. The essays are the running log; the shelf is updated when an edition changes; broken doors get repaired through contact. There is no team and no content calendar. There is one person, a lantern, and a habit of writing things down.

One honest warning

This is a large site with a lot of internal jargon, most of it from the systems work. You can ignore all of it and still read five novels. If a page starts explaining Merkle roots at you and you did not ask, that is my house being a workshop; close the tab and go back to the shelf. The glossary exists so that you can look up one word and leave, which is the correct way to use a glossary.

One more thing worth saying plainly: the house is maintained by one person on purpose. That means fewer pages, slower responses, and no content calendar. It also means that a page you bookmark will still be the page you bookmarked, which is a rarer promise than it should be. If that trade is acceptable to you, there is a chair. If you want a feed, there are better feeds.
