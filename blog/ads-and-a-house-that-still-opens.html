House

Ads and a house that still opens

I would rather a stranger finish an essay than fund a page that has nothing on it but a rectangle waiting for a network.

JH
Justin Helmer
4 September 2026 · about a twelve-minute read

Advertising networks are blunt instruments. They look at a domain and ask: is there unique writing, a human publisher, a way to contact someone, a privacy policy that is not three sentences, and a first page that is not a pile of doors to somewhere else? If the answer is no, they call it low value. Sometimes that is unfair. Sometimes it is a mirror.

Eternal Haven had essays. It also had a homepage that still behaved like a systems directory, a privacy page that was a stub, and too many interior maps in the top navigation for a guest who only wanted a chapter. That is on me. This week I am putting furniture in the rooms that should have had furniture: about, books, music, contact, a real privacy policy, terms, and new essays that could only have been written here.

Where an ad is allowed to sit

After a reader has been given something. Not on the contact page. Not as the first object in the hero. Optional. Labeled. The same rule as a magazine card: you already paid with your time; the rectangle is not the product. Support without ads remains PayPal.me/ExcavationPro and Patreon. A gift does not buy a different story.

What I will not do to “pass”

I will not generate fifty fake articles about “best VPNs.” I will not hide the games. I will not copy another site’s posts. I will not stuff keywords until the sentences die. I will not claim a review I cannot request yet. Calendars are calendars. The work is the work. If a reviewer arrives in September, they should find a house: original pages, labeled doors, a human with a name, and a lantern that does not require a login.

Thin content is a hospitality failure. Thick content that is only jargon is a different failure. The middle path is this: write like a person, keep the maps one click away, and let a stranger leave without a speech. That is the same ethic as the listen portal and the golf course. It is the only ethic that makes ads tolerable in a library.

The door has to open

An ad is not a wall. If the page stops working when the ad fails, the ad was load-bearing and the house was badly built. The test is simple: block the ad, open the door, and see whether the room still has a floor. If it does not, the problem is the architecture, not the network.

That is why the public pages keep the content and the navigation independent of the ad layer. The ad can fail; the page still has to be a page.

The house still opens

A page that survives a blocked ad is a page that was built for a reader first. That is the standard I want on every room here: the content is the structure, the ad is a curtain, and the door opens either way.
