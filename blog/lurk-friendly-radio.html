Radio

Lurk-friendly radio

I wanted a room you could enter without announcing yourself. Headphones on. Chat closed. Originals in the air. That is not a growth hack. It is hospitality.

JH
Justin Helmer · Excavationpro
31 August 2026 · about a twelve-minute read

Most live streams are designed like a stage. The host is “on.” The chat is a second stage. Even silence is a kind of performance: you are supposed to drop a fire emoji so the algorithm knows a human is in the seats. I have done that dance. It is not evil. It is also exhausting if the thing you actually wanted was music.

The coffee-room radio I keep — originals, lurk-friendly, running when I am asleep — is the opposite design. You can lurk. You can leave. You can come back three hours later and the room has not taken attendance. If that sounds small, try finding it on purpose. A lot of the internet will not let you be quiet in public.

Originals only, on purpose

The stream is Excavationpro and LYGO music: things I made, or that belong to this lattice of work. That is not a purity test against the rest of culture. It is a practical boundary. If I play other people’s catalogs I inherit their licenses, their claims, their midnight takedowns. If I play my own, the room stays mine. The listen portal is the on-demand twin of that idea: a catalog you can search, not a slot machine of other people’s hits.

People sometimes ask why not just put everything on a playlist and go to bed. I do that too. Live is different because time is shared. A radio that is “on” at 3 a.m. is a signal that someone, somewhere, left a light. You do not have to talk to it. You only have to know it exists.

A coliseum needs winners. A coffee room needs a pot that is not empty.

Monetized is not the same as captured

The Rumble room is monetized. That word makes some listeners flinch, as if money were a trapdoor under the song. I understand the flinch. A lot of “support the creator” language is a funnel with nicer lighting. Here is the honest version: a stream that is always on has a bill. If a lurk-friendly room can also be a legal, above-board way to keep the lights on, I will take that over pretending the internet is a gift economy while a platform farms the gift.

A tip, a join, a Patreon month — none of those buy the right to demand I be “on.” The room stays lurk-friendly either way. That is the deal I can keep.

How to use it, if you want a ritual

  1. Open the live room or the listen portal.
  2. Do not introduce yourself unless you feel like it.
  3. Put the volume where it sits behind your thoughts, not on top of them.
  4. If a song snags you, search the title later. If it doesn’t, let it pass.
  5. Leave without a speech. The room will not be offended.

That is the whole liturgy. I will not add a seventh step that harvests your attention. If you want television instead of music, LYGO TV is the sibling room: pick a list, click a channel, watch. Same hospitality rule. No account. No invented channels. If a stream is dead, we say so.

When I sit in that room myself, I am often doing unglamorous work: checking a hash, fixing a dead stream, writing an essay. The music is not a soundtrack for a brand. It is the thing I would be playing anyway. If you hear a track you like, the catalog is one door over. If you do not, you still got a quiet hour. That counts.
