Public data

What a public globe is for

A spinning Earth full of dots is easy to build and easy to misuse. I built one anyway, with a rule: public is a resource, private is a named shadow, and we do not invent the weather.

JH
Justin Helmer · Excavationpro
31 August 2026 · about a thirteen-minute read

If you open Public Witness you will see two geometries for the same overlay: a sphere and a north-centered disc. USGS quakes, NASA events, the ISS, a wire of public headlines. Pretty globes are not a moat. Anyone can draw a planet. The moat — if there is one — is the discipline of not filling empty space with a story you do not have.

I did not want Palantir. I did not want a “god’s eye” toy that implies you can see into rooms you have no right to see. I wanted a lantern over the part of the world that is already public: agencies publishing feeds, satellites publishing stills, journalists publishing URLs. If a feed is down, the pin stays named. If a room is private, we draw a hollow and list the legal doors around it. Payload: none.

Reference is not canon

This matters more than the 3-D. Public headlines are reference. Dual ledgers, eggs, the Star Chart — those are canon for this lattice, and they do not get silently stuffed with newspaper titles. We keep an append-only event ledger of what the Witness page actually fetched. That ledger is still a public resource, not a secret book. Confusing those layers is how a dashboard becomes a religion.

When people say “log it in the eternal ledger,” I hear two jobs. One is honest: write down what we saw, with a hash, with a time. The other is dishonest: promote a rumor into scripture because the globe looked hungry. We do the first. We refuse the second.

Empty beats fake, public orients, lattice decides. The rest is implementation.

Breaking news without becoming a wire service

The monitor on Witness pulls disaster agencies and a few world wires: GDACS, USGS, ReliefWeb, NHC, EONET, NWS, UN, WHO, and a handful of newspapers that already publish RSS. That is not “all news.” It is a sample of what is already on the sidewalk. Severe lane means disasters and a keyword filter, not a claim that we know which story is the most important on Earth.

SKYNET — as we use the word — is a sentinel on those public titles. It scores discourse, not people. It is not a weapons net. If you want television, there is LYGO TV. If you want music, there is the listen portal. Different rooms. Same rule. No account required to look.

Named shadows

The phrase sounds theatrical. It is a technical courtesy. Some nodes we will not open: ops desks, vaults, anything that would require stealing a payload. Deleting them from the map would be a different lie — the lie of a world with no locked doors. So we keep a silhouette and a why and a list of public checks. You are not owed the interior. You are owed the honesty that an interior exists.

That is the opposite of doxxing. It is the opposite of scraping doorbells. Official cameras only: NOAA, NASA, USGS pages we do not rehost. If that makes the camera wall look small, it is small on purpose.

What to do with a globe

Look. Click a pin. Read the agency page. Notice how often the honest overlay is incomplete. Then go live your day. A public globe is an orientation tool, not a lifestyle. If it becomes a lifestyle, you have slipped from witness into weather, and weather will eat your sleep.

I check it the way I check a radio: is the lantern still on, did a feed die, is there a quake I should not pretend I already understood. Then I close it. The map does not need me to perform concern. The people in the actual weather never asked for my dashboard.

If you only remember one line, remember this: empty beats fake, public orients, lattice decides. The rest is implementation.
