Contact

This is a one-person house. I read mail. I do not run a ticket queue. If you write, say which room you came from: books, music, games, or essays.

On-site form

Public contact remains @Excavationpro on X and GitHub DeepSeekOracle. No street address is published.

Please enter your name.

Please enter a valid email.

Please write a short message. Do not paste secrets.

How to reach me

Public: @Excavationpro on X. That is the fastest public door. I do not require a follow, and I do not require a performance. A short, specific note beats a pitch deck.

Support (not a support desk): PayPal.me/ExcavationPro · Patreon / Excavationpro. A payment is a gift. It does not buy a private chapter, a ranking, or a different ending.

Music distribution links for listeners: ffm.to/eovnvo9. Free listen: asiancoastline.com/listen.html.

Rights, press, and takedowns

Books and essays on this domain are © Justin Helmer. If you want to quote more than a short excerpt, ask. If you found a copy of a chapter or a stream that is not on my labeled doors, tell me the URL. I would rather fix a leak than write a speech.

I do not sell email lists. I do not run comment spam. If a page is broken, name the URL and what you expected. That is enough.

What I will not answer

Requests to invent a source, to “just generate” a ledger entry, or to auto-post on my accounts. The house rule is consent this turn. Also: I will not debug your homework, mint a token for a stranger, or pretend a 404 is a secret room.

What to put in a message

Three things make a note actionable: the URL you were on, what you expected, and what happened instead. "The book page for Book II shows a store link that 404s" is a message I can fix in ten minutes. "Your site is broken" is a message I have to guess at, and guessing is how a one-person house loses a week.

For rights requests, name the work, the use, the territory, and the term. For a correction, quote the sentence and say what it should say. For a broken chapter or an unofficial copy of a book, send the link and I will look. I would rather fix a leak than write a speech about one.

Before you write: four answers already on the site

Most mail I get is answered by a page that already exists, so here they are in one place. "Where do I start with the books?" — the start page, then Book I. "Can I use a track in a video?" — the music license; personal listening is covered, commercial sync needs a written yes. "Why do the games live somewhere else?" — the games page explains the move. "What is LYGO?" — the glossary defines it in two sentences, and the hub is the map if you want the whole thing.

How long a reply takes

Days, not hours, and longer in a busy month. There is no ticket queue and no assistant; the same person who writes the books reads the mail. If you need a same-day answer, X is faster than the form. If you need a considered answer about rights or a correction, the form is better because it gives me the URL and the detail in one place.
