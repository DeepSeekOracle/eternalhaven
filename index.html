Why this house exists

Feeds are weather. Sites are houses. I would rather own a small messy hallway than rent a clean cage, which is why this domain is a library rather than a profile. A profile is a shape a platform asks you to hold. A library is a set of shelves you can walk past, pull one thing off, and sit down with. That is what I wanted to make: a place where the work is the point and the visitor is not asked to perform anything before they can read.

So here is the honest inventory. There are five novels in one continuous saga, written over years, in order, with no volume sacrificed to a content calendar. There are essays about craft, small systems, music, and what it costs to publish without an audience machine behind you. There is a music catalog of original recordings, free to hear, with no account and no preview wall. And there is a workshop of small tools and rooms, most of which do one job and say plainly what they cannot do.

I am Justin Helmer. I publish music as Excavationpro. The systems work sometimes uses the name LYGO, and the steward role sometimes uses the name Lightfather. None of those names are required to read a chapter. They are labels on different shelves in the same building, and a first-time visitor is allowed to ignore most of them.

The rule that keeps this place honest is short: empty beats fake. If an edition does not exist, the page says it does not exist. If a feed is down, the page says it is down. If a tool is a demonstration, it is labelled a demonstration. I would rather publish a page that admits what is missing than a page that performs completeness, because the second kind of page is how a small site turns into a storefront with a story stapled to it.

The rule has a cost, and the cost is slow growth. A site that does not chase the feed does not arrive anywhere quickly. What it gets instead is a shelf that stays where you left it: a URL you can bookmark and come back to in two years, still pointing at the same book, the same essay, the same number. In a web that quietly rewrites itself every quarter, that is the only luxury I actually want to offer.

If you came for the fiction, start with The Moonlit Slumber. If you came for the argument, the essays are the argument. If you came because someone linked a tool, the tools room is one page and it will tell you what each thing does. And if you are not sure, the start page asks what you came for and points at exactly one door.

The books

Five volumes, one line, and the line matters: The Moonlit Slumber, The Shattered Accord, The Ascension War, Eternal Haven Dawns, and the ebook continuation, The Unwritten Seal. Each has a page here with a real introduction written for this domain: where the book begins, what it argues, how to read it, and what to read next.

Paperbacks are on Lulu and the narration is on YouTube, both linked from each volume's page. No affiliate links sit between you and a copy. If a storefront has rotted, write to me and I will point you at the copy that actually exists rather than the one a template says should.

If you want the map before the journey, the spoiler-light cast list and the world page will carry you through the first hundred pages without ruining anything. The shelf holds all five in order.

The essays

The essays are where I argue with my own work in public: what a page owes a reader, what a hash actually proves about a book, why a saga resists optimisation, and what a quiet website costs. They were written for this domain, they carry dates, and the ones about the house's rules are the ones I would hand to someone deciding whether to trust the place.

Music

The catalog is free to hear, and the player lives on another domain because audio is heavy: asiancoastline.com/listen.html, with a plainer backup on GitHub Pages. No account, no preview wall, no countdown. Stores carry a subset — Spotify, YouTube Music, Audius — but a store can change its terms and a domain you own cannot be delisted by a policy update.

How a song actually arrives here — mixed, encoded, hashed, published, then linked from a page that explains it — is in Music in this house. The licence is written to be read rather than survived: the music license.

Games and tools

The games are small browser things with local scorecards and no login; the playable builds live at the arcade and the games page here explains what each one is and which to open first. The tools are quieter: an ASCII art studio that runs entirely in your browser, a live chart of anchored seals, a public-feed witness that says when a feed is down instead of showing a stale number, and a room that explains each one honestly.

None of them needs an account, and none of them carries an ad. A tool that taxes its own use is a worse tool, which is a policy rather than a courtesy.

How this site is kept

Pages carry dates because pages change. In November 2026 the site got its largest single pass: the five book pages, a cast list, a world page, a glossary, a start page, a tools room, four long essays, and four rooms that used to be bare redirects. The standards page records the rules and the changelog, including what was fixed and when.

For anyone who wants the dense systems map: the hub is the workshop index, the network page is the public door, and the glossary defines the shared vocabulary one line at a time. Those pages are for people who already have a reason to be there. This one is for the visitor who does not.

There is no comments section, no newsletter, and no analytics script. Those are absences with reasons: a comment system needs moderation I cannot staff, a newsletter needs a promise I do not want to make, and analytics would mean a third-party script that the privacy page would then have to explain. If you want to know when something ships, the essay index is the changelog.

Support

Essays and tools stay free; hosting is a bill. Optional support through PayPal.me/ExcavationPro or Patreon helps keep the rooms online. A gift does not buy a different story, a private chapter, or a ranking. Advertisements, when they run, sit after you have already been given something to read — never above the title.
