    What God's Eye is

    A single iris for the LYGO lattice. Outer world public APIs, inner lattice JSON, and the rooms we will not open — all labeled. If a GET fails, the node stays on the ring with its public check-link. Live Star Chart monitor is on /starchart/; canonical ingest stays steward-gated.

    How to use it

    • Humans: click a light, read the card, open the agency or lattice page.
    • Agents: the same card is the briefing. RESOURCE vs CANON vs SHADOW. Payload on shadows is always null.
    • Earth geography lives on Witness. This eye is schematic — graph rings, not a spy satellite.

    What you are looking at

    A globe of public feeds, arranged so that the world is in one frame instead of nine tabs. The iris in the middle is a reading device: each light is a source that is currently reporting, and the ring around it is the freshness of that report. A light that dims is a feed that has gone quiet, which is information rather than an error.

    Nothing here is a prediction. The globe does not know what is about to happen, does not rank events by drama, and does not editorialise the weather. It shows what public services are publishing, with timestamps, so you can see the difference between a live reading and a stale one. The page that explains the principle is the witness room; the essay is What a public globe is for.

    How to read a light

    Start with the timestamp, not the colour. A bright light with an hour-old reading is a stale fact; a dim light with a two-minute reading is a live one. Then click it: the panel names the source and the reading, and you can check the source yourself. That is the whole method. It is deliberately unexciting, because the alternative — a dashboard that feels authoritative — is how people end up trusting a number nobody can trace.

    If a feed fails, the globe says so rather than freezing the last good value. Empty beats fake, including on a map. If you want the vocabulary, the glossary defines witness, reference, and canon in one line each.
