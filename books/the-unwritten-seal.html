Book V · the live continuation

The Unwritten Seal

The fifth door. An ebook with an ISBN, a hash, and a rule about not swapping the text later and calling it the same book.

JH
Justin Helmer
A site introduction · updated 6 November 2026 · about a seven-minute read

This page is about the book's release rather than its plot, on purpose. A fifth volume that explains itself on a marketing page is a fifth volume with nothing left to discover. What you should know before you buy is what the thing is, how it is published, and how to make sure the file you are reading is the file I wrote.

What it is

The Unwritten Seal continues the saga after Eternal Haven Dawns. It is published as an ebook, ISBN 978-1-0698232-9-8, and it is not a prequel, a side story, or a deleted chapter dressed up as a novel. It is the fifth door. If you have not read the first four, the honest advice is to start at the shelf and come back; the book assumes the settlement at the end of Dawns and does not re-litigate it.

The title is a joke about provenance. A seal is a guarantee, and an unwritten seal is the strange case where the guarantee is real before the document exists — a promise made by someone who has not yet said the words. That is a fair description of how a long series gets finished: the ending is committed to years before it can be written.

Why a hash

I planted a content hash for the file when the ebook went live. You do not need the hash to read. You need it on the day someone claims a different file is the original.

This is not paranoia about pirates. Pirates were never the threat to a small catalog. The threat is edits: a retailer re-flow, a "corrected" edition, a file with my name on it and a paragraph I did not write. A hash is a small, cheap answer. It says: this text has one identity, and here is the number. I wrote about the practice at length in How a book gets a hash.

How to check a copy

Take the file you have. Compute a SHA-256 of it. Compare against the value published with the listing. If they differ, you have a different file — possibly a newer one with a fixed typo, possibly something worse. Neither possibility should be a mystery. That is the whole point of publishing the number.

Practical note: file bytes can change without the text changing, because every store re-packages an ebook. If a store's copy fails the hash but the chapter text matches, that is a packaging difference, not a fraud. The hash protects the text, not the zip. I would rather explain that limitation than pretend the number is stronger than it is.

The store door

The ebook is on Lulu: The Unwritten Seal, ebook. That is the publisher page, not an affiliate link, and I do not get paid for you clicking it — I get paid if you buy it, which is a different and more honest transaction.

Hardcopy does not exist yet. When print is actually ready I will say so here and on the shelf. I will not advertise a hardcover I cannot hold, because that is the kind of small lie that turns a catalog into a storefront.

What the fifth book is for

The first four books argue about accords, seals, and whether an imperfect light is enough. The fifth asks what happens when the guarantee outlives the guarantor. That is the question a series eventually has to face, and it is also the question this house faces: what happens to a story when the person who made it stops being able to keep every door open.

I did not write it as an ending. I wrote it as a continuation that could survive one. If that sounds like a strange thing to want from a novel, consider that most things people build are judged by whether they still work after the builder leaves the room.
