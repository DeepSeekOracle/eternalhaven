How a song actually arrives

A folder of masters is not a release. I hash the WAV, encode a 160 kbps stream, append the public playlist, and only then does the player learn the title. Duplicate MP3s, Grok stills, and music videos do not become extra tracks. If two files are the same song under a short name and a long name, the long name wins. I wrote the longer version of this craft in Shipping a song to the open web.

Recent albums on that path: September Blues (nineteen unique masters) and BROKEN by Wind (seven). Search those titles in the player. If your browser still shows an old count, hard-refresh. Caches are weather. The playlist file is the house.

What “empty beats fake” means for a catalog

If a lyric is not in the folder, the lyrics panel stays empty. If a stream is not uploaded, the row is not painted as live. I would rather a missing panel than a lie that looks like a product. The essay Empty beats fake is the same rule in slower language.

The catalog, in numbers I can stand behind

The free listen set is on the order of ten thousand files, and a large share of the catalog carries an ISRC, which is the code that gives a recording an identity independent of whichever store happens to be carrying it. That number is not a boast; it is a logistics fact. Every one of those files had to be mixed, mastered, encoded, named, and published, and the reason the number is large is that the work was done over years rather than in a campaign.

What I will not do is inflate it. A thousand rehearsal takes are not a thousand songs, and a catalog page that counts every bounce as a release is the audio version of a site with forty thin pages. The public catalog and the sovereign hub show the ledger for anyone who wants to audit the claim.

The public ledger, and why it is public

Two pages on this domain exist to make the catalog checkable rather than describable. The catalog page lists what is in the set; the hub shows how the vault is built, what the Merkle root covers, and how retrieval works. Neither is a storefront. They are the receipts.

This matters more for a small catalog than a large one. A label with a legal department can survive an argument about who owns a master. A person with a laptop cannot, so the person with a laptop publishes numbers. If a distributor changes its terms, the ISRC and the hash still say what the recording is.

Licensing without a lawyer

Three sentences cover most of it. You may listen and share an official link. You may download copies offered for personal listening. You may not sell the tracks, bundle them as a beat pack, monetise them on a video platform, or train a commercial model on them and resell the output. Everything else — sync, games, film, advertising — needs a written yes, and the license page is written to be read rather than survived.

I answer licensing mail warmly, including the ones that begin "probably a no". A clear ask costs nothing and a quiet assumption costs both of us.
